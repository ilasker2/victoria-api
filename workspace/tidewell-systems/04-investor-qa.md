# Investor Questions: Weak and Strong Answers

> **Source:** Appendix D (15 investor questions, with weak and strong answers)
> **Type:** Worked examples and coaching rubric, **built on Tidewell's numbers** (Appendices A to C)
> **Related files:** `01-overview.md`, `02-financial-history.md`, `03-forecast.md`. For the company-neutral coaching method and investor types, see `knowledge/investor-qa.md` and `knowledge/investor-profiles.md`.

These are the questions a seed investor is most likely to ask Tidewell, in roughly the order they come up. Each has a weak answer (what the founders would say today) and a strong one built from the data in Appendix B.

## What makes an answer strong

A strong answer does four things:

1. **Gives the number.**
2. **Says how it was calculated.**
3. **Admits the weak spot.**
4. **Says what the founders are doing about it.**

## How the agent should use this file

- Use these as worked examples. The agent should be able to produce answers like the strong ones for questions that are **not** on this list.
- The strong answers cite Tidewell's figures. Use them only when coaching Tidewell, never as benchmarks for another company.
- Where the case data does not contain the evidence (see questions 13 and 15), say so and help the founder find it. Do not invent it.

## Traction and revenue

### 1. What is your ARR, and how do you calculate it?

**Why they ask:** To see if the founder knows the difference between recurring and one-time revenue.

**Weak:** "$1.37M." (September's total revenue times 12, which includes $7,200 of one-time onboarding fees.)

**Strong:** "$1.29M. That's September MRR of $107,189 times 12. We leave out onboarding fees because they don't repeat. They were $108,600 over the last 12 months."

### 2. How fast are you growing?

**Why they ask:** Growth rate drives almost every other question, including how much the round is worth to them.

**Weak:** "10% month over month."

**Strong:** "MRR went from $38K to $107K in the 12 months to September, about 9% a month compounded. About $10K of that is one customer, Northern Comfort Group, signed in March. Without them it's about 8%. Growth slowed to about 5% a month from July to September, and here is why we think that happened."

### 3. What is your churn?

**Why they ask:** SMB software lives or dies on churn. High churn means growth has to work harder every month just to stand still.

**Weak:** "Around 1%. Most of it was early customers on the old product."

**Strong:** "We lost 2.0% of customers a month over the last 12 months, about a fifth of the base a year. The last six months were better, at 1.6%. Most churn happens early: customers on monthly plans churn at about 3.3% a month in their first six months and about 1.4% after. So we're putting our onboarding effort into the first 90 days."

### 4. What is your net revenue retention?

**Why they ask:** NRR shows whether existing customers grow or shrink over time, which tells an investor how much growth comes for free.

**Weak:** "Above 110%."

**Strong:** "Customers who were paying us $38.0K a month in September 2025 pay $33.3K today, so NRR is about 88% and gross retention about 81%. Seat expansion is small because our customers hire technicians slowly. That's the number we most want to improve, and the price increase plan is part of it."

## Unit economics

### 5. What is your gross margin?

**Why they ask:** Margin decides how much of each dollar of revenue can pay for growth.

**Weak:** "92%."

**Strong:** "92% if you only count hosting, SMS and card fees. Most SaaS investors also count customer support, and on that basis it's 74% over the last 12 months. Our three customer success staff cost $17,000 a month against $114,000 of September revenue."

### 6. What does it cost you to win a customer, and how long to earn it back?

**Why they ask:** CAC and payback show whether more sales and marketing money turns into profitable growth.

**Weak:** "$970, paid back in under 3 months." (Marketing programs only, divided by new customers.)

**Strong:** "Fully loaded, about $3,200 per customer. That's $491,000 of sales and marketing salaries, commissions and programs over 153 new customers in the last 12 months. A new customer brings about $458 of MRR, so payback is about 9 to 10 months on a margin that includes support."

## The forecast

### 7. Your forecast assumes churn drops to 1% and a 20% price increase with no extra churn. Why?

**Why they ask:** A forecast is only as credible as its weakest assumption. Investors test the ones that move the result most.

**Weak:** "We're confident in the product."

**Strong:** "Fair challenge. Churn has been 2.0% a month, and 1.6% recently, so 1% is a target, not a trend. We have a version at 2% churn. On price, our annual customers can't be repriced until renewal, and 59 customers are still on the legacy $39 price. We'd phase it in and expect some churn. Here's what the plan looks like if both go against us."

### 8. How fast do new account executives ramp, and how many deals will they close?

**Why they ask:** The hiring plan only works if new reps produce. Ramp time is where most seed forecasts break.

**Weak:** "One month. The product is easy to demo."

**Strong:** "Our two reps took about three months to get to 4 or 5 new customers a month. The plan assumes 5 a month after one month. We should move that to a three-month ramp, and we'll show you the revised numbers."

### 9. How dependent are you on Northern Comfort Group?

**Why they ask:** One customer can make a small company look bigger than it is, and a renewal can go either way.

**Weak:** "They're expanding to 600 seats next year."

**Strong:** "They're about 10% of MRR. They renew in March 2027. Their COO has said they want every location on Tidewell, but nothing is signed, so the base plan should keep them at 260 seats and show the expansion as upside."

## Cash and the raise

### 10. Why $4M, and how long does it last?

**Why they ask:** To judge whether the founders understand their burn and have planned for the next round.

**Weak:** "24 months of runway."

**Strong:** "On our plan, cash lasts to about November 2028, so roughly 22 months after a January close. If growth stays at the rates we've actually achieved, it's closer to 17 months, which means we'd start raising again in late 2027. Here are the milestones we'd need to hit and where we'd slow hiring if we miss them."

### 11. What happens if the round takes longer than you expect?

**Why they ask:** Founders who run out of money mid-raise lose their negotiating position.

**Weak:** "It won't. We have strong interest."

**Strong:** "We have $483K and have been burning about $58K a month, so about 8 months. Our plan raises marketing spend from October, before the money lands. If the round slipped to April, that plan runs out of cash in March. So we're holding spending at today's level until a term sheet is signed."

### 12. Your deck says $2B TAM. Where does that come from?

**Why they ask:** A large unsourced number tells an investor the founder hasn't done the work.

**Weak:** "Industry reports."

**Strong:** "We haven't built it bottom-up yet, so we've taken that number off the slide. Our method is the number of trades businesses with 3 to 30 technicians in Canada and the US, times average seats, times our price. We'll source the business counts from Statistics Canada and the US Census Bureau."

## Strategy

### 13. Why will you win against Jobber, Housecall Pro and ServiceTitan?

**Why they ask:** To see whether the founder knows why customers choose them.

**Weak:** "Our product is better."

**Strong:** "We don't track win and loss reasons in a system yet, which we're fixing. What we hear from customers is X and Y. Here are three customers who switched from a competitor and what they told us."

**Note for the agent:** The data pack does not include competitor or win/loss data. A good agent says so and helps the founder find evidence rather than inventing it.

### 14. What will this round get you to, and what is the biggest risk?

**Why they ask:** Investors fund a path to the next round, not a budget.

**Weak:** "Series A at $5M ARR."

**Strong:** "Our plan gets to about $5.1M ARR by March 2028. The milestones that matter for a Series A are churn under a stated target, a repeatable rep ramp, and Northern Comfort renewed. The biggest risk is that growth stays near 5% a month while we triple the team. If churn or rep productivity miss for two quarters, we slow hiring."

### 15. What valuation are you looking for?

**Why they ask:** To see whether the founder's expectations are realistic, and sometimes to anchor the negotiation.

**Weak:** Naming a number with no reasoning, or "whatever the market says" with no preparation.

**Strong:** "We'd like the lead to propose terms. Here is what we know: our metrics, the round size, and the two SAFEs that convert at this round with $10M and $15M caps."

**Note for the agent:** The agent can explain how valuation and dilution work and how the SAFEs convert. **It must not recommend a valuation.** That is a requirement in the challenge brief.
