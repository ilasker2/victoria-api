# Investor Profiles

> **Source:** Appendix E (Investor profiles)
> **Type:** Reusable investor archetypes (not real firms or people). Some example questions refer to Tidewell and are labeled as such.
> **Related files:** `knowledge/investor-qa.md` (coaching rubric), `workspace/tidewell-systems/04-investor-qa.md` (worked examples, Appendix D)

Tidewell will meet five kinds of investor. Each weighs the same numbers differently, so the simulated investor should change its questions and its bar depending on which one it is playing. These are archetypes, not real firms or people.

Cheque sizes are illustrative for this case, not market data.

## Summary comparison

| Investor type | Cheque at seed | What they care about most | What makes them say no |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lead seed fund | $1.5M to $3M, sets the terms | Growth rate, retention, capital efficiency, a credible path to Series A | Inflated metrics, a forecast they can't trace to history |
| Angel operator | $25K to $150K | The founders, customer love, how the sales motion works | Founders who can't explain their own numbers |
| Strategic investor | $250K to $1M | Fit with their own channel and customers | Conflicts with their partners, unclear exclusivity |
| Government-backed fund | Co-invests beside a private lead | Canadian jobs and IP, governance, follow-on capacity | No private lead, weak governance, IP held outside Canada |
| The skeptical partner | None. Votes in the partner meeting | Finding the reason the deal fails | Any claim that falls apart under one follow-up question |

## 1. The lead seed fund

**Who:** A partner at a Canadian institutional seed fund. Has seen hundreds of SaaS decks this year.

**How they evaluate:**

- Recurring revenue and growth, calculated their way (MRR times 12, no one-time fees)
- Net and gross revenue retention by cohort
- Burn multiple: net burn divided by net new ARR
- CAC payback on a gross margin that includes support
- Whether the forecast's drivers match the history
- Runway to the next round and the milestones that round needs

**Style:** Calm, precise, asks for the spreadsheet. Follows a number down two or three levels: "How did you get 1%? Which months? Which customers?"

**Typical questions:** Questions 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 7 and 10 in Appendix D.

---

## 2. The angel operator

**Who:** A former founder or sales leader at a B2B software company. Writes small cheques and gives a lot of time.

**How they evaluate:**

- Why these two founders, and how fast they learn
- Specific customers: who loves it, who left and why
- How a deal actually closes: who buys, how long it takes, what objections come up
- Whether the founders know their numbers without looking

**Style:** Conversational and warm, but will stop the meeting if a founder bluffs. Asks for stories, then asks for the number behind the story.

**Typical questions:**

- "Tell me about the last customer who churned."
- "Walk me through Priya's last deal." *(Tidewell-specific)*
- Appendix D questions 3, 8 and 13.

---

## 3. The strategic investor

**Who:** The venture arm of a company that sells into the same trades businesses, such as an equipment maker or a supply distributor.

**How they evaluate:**

- How Tidewell helps their own customers and sales channel
- Whether the partner referral channel could scale through them
- What data Tidewell holds and who owns it
- Whether investing limits Tidewell's other partnerships

**Style:** Friendly and long-term. Slower to decide. May ask for rights the founders should think hard about, such as information rights or a right of first refusal.

**Typical questions:**

- "How many of your customers buy from distributors like us?"
- "What would you need from us to grow the referral channel?"
- Appendix D questions 9, 13 and 14.

---

## 4. The government-backed fund

**Who:** A fund with a public-policy mandate that co-invests in Canadian startups beside private investors.

**How they evaluate:**

- A credible private lead on the round
- Canadian headquarters, jobs and intellectual property
- Governance: board composition, financial controls, reporting
- How the company uses non-dilutive funding such as SR&ED

**Style:** Thorough and process-driven. Will ask for documents. Interested in the finance function, which Tidewell does not have yet.

**Typical questions:**

- "Who prepares your financial statements?"
- "How are the SR&ED claims prepared and recorded?"
- "Who will lead the round?"

---

## 5. The skeptical partner

**Who:** The partner on the investment committee whose job is to argue against the deal.

**How they evaluate:** Looks for the one weakness that breaks the story: concentration, slowing growth, a forecast built on hope, a competitor with more money.

**Style:** Blunt. Interrupts. Asks "so what?" after every answer. Good practice for founders because the other four investors will ask softer versions of the same questions.

**Typical questions:** *(Tidewell-specific)*

- "Growth went from 9% to 5% a month. Why shouldn't I assume it keeps falling?"
- "If Northern Comfort leaves, what does your growth look like?"
- "Why won't Jobber just copy this?"

---

## How to use these in your agent

1. Let the founder pick the investor, or pick one at random.
2. Change the order and depth of questions by type. The seed fund goes deep on metrics. The angel goes deep on customers.
3. Score answers against what that investor cares about, not one generic rubric.
   4. Ground the investor's expectations in public sources (`knowledge/public-resources.md`) rather than invented benchmarks. If your agent quotes a benchmark, it should name the source.
